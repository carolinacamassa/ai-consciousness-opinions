About

This site collects what people on X say about whether AI systems are conscious, and about whether they are owed moral consideration, including arguments for or against that don't depend on consciousness. Each opinion is sorted into one of three stances:

    {% for s in stances %}
  • {{ s.name }} {{ s.blurb }}
    • {% endfor %}

An opinion is an original tweet or a quote tweet. When its author continued it in a thread, the whole thread counts as one opinion. Replies to other people are not collected. Only human opinions are kept: tweets that just relay what a model said about itself, news without comment, and questions are left out.

The tweets come from the Community Archive, an opt-in archive of X, starting {{ start_date }}. The share of each stance describes the accounts in that archive, which lean toward particular corners of AI Twitter, not public opinion.

How it works

  1. The archive is searched for {% for label in searches %}“{{ label }}”{{ ", " if not loop.last }}{% endfor %} and related phrases.
  2. Each match is read together with the tweet it quotes, the rest of its thread and its attached images. {{ classifier }} decides whether it is a human opinion on the topic, which stance it takes, and how much substance it has, from 1 to 10. Opinions scoring {{ min_score }} or more appear on the site. Stances are assigned per opinion, not per person, and the grader can be wrong.
  3. Each opinion on the site is tagged with up to three of the arguments it makes, from a fixed list (version {{ args_version }}), by {{ tagger }}. The list came from two passes of {{ summarizer }} over all the opinions' claims; when it changes, every opinion is tagged again.
  4. Opinions are ranked by {{ rank_quote }} × quotes + {{ rank_rt }} × log2(1 + min(retweets, {{ rank_cap }})) + {{ rank_score }} × substance, where quotes counts distinct Community Archive accounts that quote-tweeted the opinion.
  5. {{ summarizer }} reads each stance's strongest opinions, at most four per account, and writes the summary on its page.
